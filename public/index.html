Monthly Cash Flow
▼ What does this mean?
The money left over each month after collecting rent and paying every expense including your mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, and property management.
Cash Flow = Effective Rent − All Expenses − Mortgage
✓ Positive = good $0 = break even Negative = losing money monthly
CA tip: Many Sacramento properties have negative cash flow today due to high prices and rates. Appreciation may still make it worth it long term.
--
Cap Rate
▼ What does this mean?
Measures a property's income potential independent of financing — what it earns as if you paid all cash.
Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Purchase Price × 100
✓ 6%+ Strong 4–6% Moderate Below 4% Weak
CA tip: Sacramento cap rates typically run 4–6%. Coastal CA markets often run 2–4%.
--
Cash-on-Cash Return
▼ What does this mean?
How much annual cash income you earn relative to the actual cash you invested. The most relevant return metric for leveraged investors.
CoC = Annual Cash Flow ÷ Total Cash Invested × 100
✓ 8%+ Strong 4–8% Moderate Below 4% Weak
8% CoC means for every $100K invested, you earn $8K/yr in cash.
--
DSCR
▼ What does this mean?
Debt Service Coverage Ratio — the ratio of your property's income to its debt payments. Lenders use this to qualify investment property loans.
DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Annual Mortgage Payments
✓ 1.25+ Lender approved 1.0–1.25 Tight Below 1.0 Won't qualify
Most CA lenders require DSCR of 1.20–1.25 for investment property loans.
--
Monthly Mortgage
▼ What does this mean?
Your principal and interest payment each month. Does not include taxes, insurance, or HOA.
P&I = Loan × [r(1+r)^n] ÷ [(1+r)^n − 1]
✓ Under 40% of rent = healthy Over 60% of rent = cash flow risk
--
Annual NOI
▼ What does this mean?
Net Operating Income — your annual income after all operating expenses, but before mortgage payments. The core profitability number used by investors and appraisers.
NOI = Effective Gross Income − Operating Expenses
Operating expenses include taxes, insurance, HOA, maintenance, and management — but NOT the mortgage.
--
Gross Rent Multiplier
▼ What does this mean?
How many years of gross rent it takes to pay for the property. Lower is better.
GRM = Purchase Price ÷ Annual Gross Rent
✓ Under 15 = strong value 15–20 = moderate Over 20 = pricey
Sacramento GRMs typically run 14–18. Bay Area can exceed 25.
--
Total Cash In
▼ What does this mean?
The total cash you need at closing — your down payment plus estimated closing costs.
Total Cash = Down Payment + Closing Costs
CA closing costs typically run 2–3% for buyers. Includes title, escrow, lender fees, and prepaid items.
